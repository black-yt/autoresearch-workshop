01 / OVERVIEW

Beyond a
successful run.

Research agents can plan, code, experiment, and revise. How do we tell when a complete workflow has produced useful, reproducible research?

Auto Research focuses on language-model agents working across the research process. We study the connections between stages: how an agent carries evidence forward, recovers from failed experiments, uses feedback, and decides what to do next.

Our goal is to connect agent builders, evaluation researchers, research-software communities, and researchers working on human–AI collaboration. Scientific tasks offer demanding test cases for general questions about planning, learning, memory, tool use, and oversight.

Planned community outputs

A research-trajectory reporting checklist, a taxonomy of failures, and an open evaluation agenda.

02 / RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Four questions.
One research loop.

Contributions may study an entire workflow or a component with a clear role in end-to-end research.

01

What sustains progress?

Long-horizon planning, tool use, memory, multi-agent coordination, recovery, and stopping decisions under explicit time and compute budgets.

Execution · Adaptation · Research coding
02

What counts as evidence?

Independent evaluation, implementation fidelity, baselines, reruns, novelty, and the link between an executed experiment and a reported conclusion.

Evaluation · Reproducibility · Provenance
03

Where do humans help?

Task specification, expert time, intervention frequency, recovery assistance, and accountable collaboration between researchers and agents.

Human–AI collaboration · Oversight
04

When is improvement real?

Cross-task learning, rubric induction, research-idea evaluation, citation grounding, reward hacking, and tests that survive changes to the agent.

Self-improvement · Validity · Failure analysis

Proposed review process

Non-archival, double-blind reviewing on OpenReview, with at least three human reviews per full paper and two per shorter paper. All accepted contributions receive a poster opportunity; at least one contributed talk is reserved for tiny papers.

Page limits exclude references, optional appendices, and AI-use disclosures. Main texts should be self-contained. Previously published papers cannot be submitted unchanged; follow-up work must identify its new contribution.

Evidence and accountability

Report model versions, data and task access, time and compute budgets, attempted runs, human interventions, and validation procedures. Shareable code and representative trajectories are encouraged.

AI-generated papers are not accepted. Submissions require accountable human authors; all shorter formats must be primarily human-authored. AI assistance must be disclosed. Conflicted organizers do not assess submissions from their own organizations or collaborators.

04 / PROGRAM

A day for discussion,
not just demonstrations.

The proposed program combines invited perspectives, contributed work, posters, open questions, and an evidence clinic. Speakers will be announced after their participation is confirmed.

The evidence clinic

Bring a trajectory.
Leave with a better test.

Small groups inspect a volunteered research record: the goal, access conditions, an experiment trace, a result, and an unresolved concern.

Together, we ask what the evidence supports, which explanations remain, and what independent check would be most informative.

A 30-minute clinic is planned within the second poster session. Precomputed traces make participation possible without live GPU or paid API access.
